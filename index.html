How to Play Scopa: Quick Rules Summary

Scopa (Italian for broom) is a traditional fishing card game played with a 40-card Italian deck divided into four suits: Denari (Coins), Coppe (Cups), Spade (Swords), and Bastoni (Clubs).

  1. The Deal: Each player is dealt 3 cards, and 4 cards are placed face-up on the table.
  2. Capturing: On your turn, play one card. If your card matches the face value of a table card, you must capture it. If no single card matches, you may capture a combination of table cards whose values sum up to your card (e.g., a 7 captures a 4 and a 3). Matching a single card is compulsory over a combination.
  3. Trailing: If your card cannot capture, it is placed face-up on the table.
  4. The Scopa (Sweep): If your capture clears the entire table, you score a Scopa (+1 point). The scoring card is turned face-up in your trick pile to track sweeps.

Scopa Scoring & Winning Points

At the end of each round, points are calculated across five traditional scoring categories:

  • Carte (Cards): 1 point to the player who captured 21 or more cards (most cards).
  • Denari (Coins): 1 point to the player who captured 6 or more coin cards (most coins).
  • Settebello (The Beautiful Seven): 1 point to the player who captured the coveted 7 of Coins.
  • Primiera: 1 point for the best four-card combination (one card of highest primiera value per suit: 7=21, 6=18, Ace=16, 5=15, 4=14, 3=13, 2=12, Face cards=10).
  • Scope (Sweeps): 1 point for every sweep completed during round play (excluding sweeps on the final play).

The first player to reach the target score (typically 11, 16, or 21 points) and hold a lead wins the match!

How to Play Briscola: Trump Suit & 120-Point Trick-Taking

Briscola is Italy's classic trick-taking card game played with the same 40-card deck. Each player receives 3 cards, and the next card is turned face up perpendicular under the stock to set the Briscola (trump suit).

  • No Obligation to Follow Suit: On each two-card trick, the second player may play any card from their hand—there is no requirement to follow the led suit or play a trump.
  • Card Strength & Points: From strongest to weakest within a suit: Ace (11 pts), Three (10 pts), King (4 pts), Knight (3 pts), Jack (2 pts), followed by 7, 6, 5, 4, and 2 (0 pts).
  • Winning the Hand: Out of 120 total deck points, capturing 61 or more points wins the hand (60–60 is a draw). Play a Single Game, Best of 3, or Best of 5! Read our complete Briscola rules and strategy guide.
